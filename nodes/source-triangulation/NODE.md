# Source Triangulation

## Purpose

Increase reliability by comparing independent evidence and making the boundary between observation, inference, and hypothesis visible.

## Process

1. Break the deliverable into atomic claims rather than evaluating whole paragraphs.
2. Classify each claim as direct website observation, official API evidence, timestamped public-web discovery, first-party connected data, or hypothesis.
3. Record source URL, publisher, access context, date, relevant excerpt or field, and known limitations.
4. Prefer independent corroboration. Check whether sources are genuinely independent, current, and measuring the same thing.
5. Resolve conflicts explicitly: retain both observations, explain the scope or timing difference, and avoid averaging incompatible measures.
6. Assign confidence from source quality and corroboration, not from how polished or certain the prose sounds.
7. Never convert absence of evidence into a negative fact. State what was not checked and what source would close the gap.

## Output standard

Return a claim-to-source ledger, corroboration status, confidence, limitations, and recommended validation queue. Keep recommendations traceable to the evidence they depend on.
